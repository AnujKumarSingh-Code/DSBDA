Absolutely! Here's a line-by-line explanation of the Titanic dataset code using `pandas`, `seaborn`, and `matplotlib`:

---

## 📌 **Part 1: Load the Titanic Dataset**

```python
import pandas as pd
import seaborn as sns
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
```
### ✅ What it does:
- `pandas`: For loading and handling the CSV data (`DataFrame`).
- `seaborn`: For easy and attractive data visualization.
- `matplotlib.pyplot`: The underlying plotting engine that seaborn uses.

---

```python
df = pd.read_csv('titanic.csv')  # Replace with your actual CSV path
```
### ✅ What it does:
- Loads the Titanic dataset into a variable called `df` (a DataFrame).
- `df` now contains all the rows and columns from the CSV file (like `PassengerId`, `Survived`, `Pclass`, `Fare`, etc.).

---

```python
print(df.head())
```
### ✅ What it does:
- Prints the **first 5 rows** of the dataset to get a quick preview of the data.

---

## 📌 **Part 2: Plot Histogram of Ticket Fare**

```python
plt.figure(figsize=(8, 5))
```
### ✅ What it does:
- Creates a blank figure with a custom size (8 inches wide, 5 inches tall).

---

```python
sns.histplot(data=df, x='Fare', kde=True, bins=30, color='teal')
```
### ✅ What it does:
- Plots a histogram of the `Fare` column (i.e. ticket prices).
- `kde=True`: Adds a **smoothed density curve** on top of the histogram (kernel density estimation).
- `bins=30`: Divides the fare range into 30 equal-width bins (bars).
- `color='teal'`: Sets the color of the bars.

---

```python
plt.title('Distribution of Ticket Fare')
plt.xlabel('Fare')
plt.ylabel('Number of Passengers')
plt.grid(True)
plt.show()
```
### ✅ What it does:
- Adds a title, x-label, y-label, and grid lines to the plot.
- Finally, `plt.show()` displays the complete histogram.

---

## ✅ Bonus: Boxplot (Optional Extra Visualization)

```python
plt.figure(figsize=(8, 5))
sns.boxplot(data=df, x='Pclass', y='Fare', hue='Survived')
plt.title('Fare Distribution by Class and Survival')
plt.show()
```

### ✅ What it does:
- `boxplot` shows how ticket fare is spread across different passenger classes (`Pclass`) and whether they survived (`Survived`).
- `hue='Survived'` creates separate boxplots for passengers who survived (1) vs those who did not (0).

### 📊 **1. Histogram of Ticket Fare**
![Histogram](attachment:file-6bW2CtSsV9mtq4CaCFTEWV)

#### What it shows:
- This **histogram** plots the distribution of ticket fares paid by passengers.
- The **x-axis** represents the fare amount.
- The **y-axis** shows the number of passengers who paid that fare range.
- A **KDE (Kernel Density Estimation)** curve is overlaid, which provides a smoothed line that estimates the probability density function of the fare distribution.

#### Interpretation:
- Most passengers paid **low fares**, clustering around the 0–50 range.
- There are **few passengers** who paid **very high fares** (above 200 or even up to 500), indicating outliers or first-class travelers.
- The distribution is **right-skewed**, meaning a small number of passengers paid disproportionately high fares.

---

### 📦 **2. Boxplot of Fare by Passenger Class and Survival**
![Boxplot](attachment:file-PDfJLu21wGtQBnYHWck8NV)

#### What it shows:
- This **boxplot** compares ticket fares across **passenger classes (Pclass)** and **survival status**.
- The **x-axis** represents the passenger class (1, 2, 3).
- The **y-axis** is the fare.
- **Hue = Survived**: The boxplot is split into two for each class—one for passengers who survived (`Survived = 1`, orange) and one for those who didn’t (`Survived = 0`, blue).

#### Interpretation:
- **1st class passengers** paid significantly higher fares than 2nd or 3rd class.
- In all classes, **survivors** tend to have slightly higher median fares than non-survivors.
- The presence of **many outliers**, especially in 1st class, indicates that some wealthy passengers paid exceptionally high fares.
- **Survival rate seems correlated with class and fare**: Passengers in 1st class who paid more had a better chance of survival.
